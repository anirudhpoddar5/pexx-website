# PEXX Journal Content Engine

One run = **one drop = three deliverables**:

1. **Bulk journal post** → poddarexp.com (buyer audience)
2. **Retail journal post** → shop.poddarexp.com (gifting + self-use audience)
3. **LinkedIn post** → derived from whichever of the two is the day's lead

Everything lands in `content-drafts/journal/YYYY-MM-DD/` for review. **Nothing is published without the owner saying so** — publishing is Section 8, and it is a separate, explicit step.

The strategy behind this skill (audience split, demand research, photo audit, calendar) is the plan published 22 Aug 2026. This file is the executable version of it.

---

## 1. Never do these

- **Never invent a lead time, MOQ, certification, GSM, price or dimension.** Pull from `references/facts.md`, an existing post, or ask. A wrong lead time in a buyer post costs an order.
- **Never claim GOTS certification.** PEXX/Poddar is pursuing GOTS + OEKO-TEX; neither is held today. Write "azo-free dyes", "organic-cotton standard", never "GOTS-certified".
- **Go easy on certifications, audits and the factory-compliance angle.** Standing owner instruction. Answer it when a buyer would genuinely ask — one FAQ line, one clause in a lead-time post — and move on. No posts built around GOTS/OEKO-TEX, no audit explainers. The interesting story is the making, not the paperwork.
- **Never put an AI-generated image on poddarexp.com, in any process/authenticity post, or on LinkedIn.** See `references/images.md` §Tier C. The sites publish "how to spot a genuine block print" — a rendered workshop destroys that page's credibility along with its own.
- **Never use "handmade with love", "curated", "elevate", "timeless elegance", "in today's fast-paced world".** Every competitor already says these.
- **Never publish a photo straight from the library.** Every frame is edited first — `references/images.md` §Pipeline. This is a standing owner instruction, not a nicety.
- **Never write the same post for both properties.** Same expertise, two rooms. A buyer wants MOQ; a mother wants to know if it survives the wash.
- **Never ship a post without the answer block and the FAQ block.** Those two are what gets quoted by AI answer engines, and they are the entire AEO strategy.
- **Never age-restrict a product** ("newborns only") or frame print variation as a flaw.

## 2. Topics are chosen by the owner, every time — this is a hard gate

**Never start writing on a topic you picked yourself.** Standing owner instruction (22 Aug 2026): he selects from suggestions before any drafting happens.

1. Read `references/calendar.md` for the slate, the cadence (one drop every 3 days), and what has shipped.
2. Research first, then **offer 3 bulk options and 3 retail options** — each one line: the title, the question it answers, and why now. Rank them, say which you'd pick, and wait for his call. If he names a topic outright, that wins and you skip straight to writing.
3. **Check the clock before offering anything.** Seasonal posts are worthless late: gifting posts publish 6–8 weeks before the occasion, trade-fair posts before the fair, winter bedding as the weather turns. If the calendar's next topic has gone stale, say so and propose the swap rather than shipping a dead post.
4. Once he has chosen, research that topic properly — do not write from memory. Minimum: one search on what people actually ask (the query, not the topic), one on the current numbers. Every factual claim in the post needs a source you actually read this session. Add any durable fact you find to `references/facts.md`.

## 3. Who is actually reading this

**The bulk journal is not read by procurement departments.** Most of our buyers
are **women running their own stores** who also buy in bulk, plus Indian
wholesale buyers. She chose every product on her shelf herself. She has been
made to feel small by a supplier before, and she is half expecting it again.

Write to her, peer to peer. Warm, direct, no corporate register, no
"leverage"/"solutions"/"partner ecosystem". The retail journal is read by someone
mid-decision with a ceremony this weekend and a phone in one hand.

Both of them should finish a paragraph thinking *this is exactly what I was
worried about*. Before drafting, write down the three things the reader is
privately anxious about and make sure the post answers all three by name.

## 4. Voice

Warm, specific, confident, human. Written by someone who makes the thing, for someone deciding about it. Positive, never robotic, never precious.

- **Lead with the consequence, not the mechanism.** "You cannot pay to make the sun work faster" beats "drying is a rate-limited process step".
- **One concrete scene early.** The October phone call. The 2am blanket misery. The grandmother's dohar. This is what separates the drafts that get read from listicles.
- **Numbers make it credible; scenes make it read.** Use both — a paragraph with neither is filler and should be cut.
- **Short sentences win.** No exclamation marks. No rhetorical throat-clearing before the answer.
- **Give something away for free** in every post — one piece of advice that helps even someone who buys elsewhere ("put the logo on the card, not the cloth"). This is what earns the share.
- **Close warm, not salesy.** A soft last line about the object in someone's home beats a CTA.
- Messaging order on the retail side: print/colour → skin-safe → made-to-keep → honest price → craft last.
- Messaging order on the bulk side: what it costs you in time/risk → how we handle it → proof → craft.
- **Warm and emotional beats efficient and complete.** Name the feeling before
  the fact — "you are half expecting to be told you're too small" lands where
  "we accommodate small order quantities" does not.
- **Never sound like AI.** No "it's not just X, it's Y". No three-item rhythms
  everywhere. No "in today's world", "elevate", "curated", "seamless",
  "delve", "landscape", "testament". Vary sentence length hard — a three-word
  sentence after a long one. Use contractions. Cut any sentence that could
  appear in any other brand's post unchanged.
- **Multiple CTAs on retail posts**, woven in where the reader is already
  convinced — after the "what gets used" section, after the occasion they came
  for, and at the end. One CTA at the bottom is a wasted post.
- **Say how we solve the problem**, concretely: gift pouch included over ₹1,999,
  gift note in their words, azo-free dyes, free shipping over ₹750, COD, 10-day
  returns, bulk from ten pieces. These are the reasons someone buys today.
- Skip spend tables and price bands on retail gifting posts unless the owner
  asks for them.

## 5. Structure every post the same way (AEO)

This structure is why the posts get cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity and AI Overviews. Do not improvise it.

1. **Title** — the question a person types, or the answer to it. Not clever.
2. **Answer block** — 40–60 words, factual, declarative, no hedging, immediately under the title. It must make sense lifted out of the page on its own, because that is exactly what happens to it.
3. **A scene** — 2–3 short paragraphs. Human, specific, no selling.
4. **The body** — H2s phrased the way people search ("What GSM should a dohar be?"). One idea per section. At least one comparison table or numbered list; tables get quoted more than prose.
5. **The free advice** — the thing you'd tell someone who isn't buying from you.
6. **FAQ block** — 4–5 questions in natural language, each answered in 2–3 declarative sentences that stand alone. These become FAQ structured data on both properties.
7. **Internal links** — 2–3, to a collection, a product, or the sibling post. Bulk posts link to the enquiry page; retail posts link to a collection.

Target 900–1,300 words. Longer is not better; unanswered is worse.

## 6. What makes the two posts different

| | poddarexp.com (bulk) | shop.poddarexp.com (retail) |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | Importer, retail chain, e-comm buyer, gifting agency, private label — often overseas | Indian urban buyer, NRI gift-sender |
| They're deciding | whether to send an enquiry | what to buy, or how to stop worrying about a gift |
| Pillars | range & trend intelligence · how an order runs (MOQ, sampling, lead time, QC) · craft at scale | occasion gifting · help me choose · care & keep · print stories |
| Proof that works | dates, quantities, tolerances, named process steps, photographs of the table | GSM numbers, wash instructions, what fits which city and season |
| Never | consumer listicles, emoji, "shop now" | export jargon, MOQ, incoterms |
| CTA | "Tell us what you're planning" → enquiry | "See the collection" → collection page |
| Category field | `Sourcing`, `Trends`, `Craft` | n/a (Shopify blog) |

## 7. The LinkedIn post

One post per drop, from the **owner's personal profile** (reaches several times further than the company page; buyers connect to people). The company page reshares.

- Pick the day's lead — usually the bulk post, unless the retail post carries the better idea.
- Three rails, roughly 40% bulk / 35% thought leadership / 25% gifting. Vary them across drops; two bulk posts in a row reads as a catalogue.
- **Hook is one line and must survive the "see more" fold.** A number, a date, a contradiction. "Diwali is 78 days away. If you want handmade gifts, that is not a lot of time."
- 150–250 words. Single-line paragraphs. No hashtag soup — two, at most, and only if genuinely used in the trade.
- **Link goes in the first comment, never the body** — links in the body suppress reach.
- One image: 1200×1200, Tier A or edited Tier B, never a render. Or a document post (carousel, 1080×1350, 8–10 slides, one idea per slide) when the post is a comparison or a list.
- End with the free advice, not the CTA. The CTA is the comment.

## 8. Images

Read `references/images.md` before choosing anything. In short: the library is `~/Desktop/PEXX/photo-library`, ~3,100 stills in 30 folders, sorted into four tiers. Tier A (process photography) is the only publish-anywhere tier. Tier B (catalogue frames on green turf with a label card) needs the full edit. Tier C is renders — retail only. Tier D is watermarked and unusable.

Per post, output an **image brief**, not a file dump: 1 hero + 2–3 in-line, each with the source path, the crop, the treatment, and the caption. If nothing in the library fits, say so and name the shot that's missing — do not force a wrong photo into a good post.

## 9. Output, then publishing

Write the drop to `content-drafts/journal/<YYYY-MM-DD>/`:

```
bulk-<slug>.md         # front matter + post body + FAQ + image brief
shop-<slug>.md         # same shape
linkedin-<slug>.md     # the post, plus the first comment
drop-notes.md          # sources used, facts added, what needs the owner's call
```

Formats, field-by-field, are in `references/formats.md`. Then **stop and hand the drafts over.** Publishing is a separate instruction, and it differs per property:

- **poddarexp.com** — run `scripts/new_bulk_post.py` (it writes `posts/<slug>.html`, the `data/posts.json` entry, and the `blog/<slug>/index.html` shell in one go), then commit. Never hand-edit `data/posts.json`; the script keeps the three in sync.
- **shop.poddarexp.com** — the Shopify theme already has `blog.json` / `article.json` templates. Create the article through the Admin API or paste it in; either way the owner approves first.
- **LinkedIn** — always hand-posted by the owner. Never automate.

## 10. Sharing it — the drop isn't finished at publish

Every post ships with a **PEXX-branded share card** rendered from
`assets/share-card.html` (fill in five fields, screenshot at the size you need —
1080×1350, 1200×1200, 1200×630). Photo must be Tier A or edited Tier B.

Then the forty minutes in `references/distribution.md`: submit the URL to Search
Console and Bing by hand, LinkedIn from the personal profile with the link in
the first comment, WhatsApp Status and buyer notes, Instagram carousel for
retail posts, Pinterest for anything about gifting or bedding, and two genuine
answers on Quora or Reddit where the question is already being asked.

Free full republishing, 24–48 hours after our own site: Medium via **Import
Story** (it sets the canonical back to us automatically), a LinkedIn article
with a changed title, and a Flipboard magazine. Our site always publishes first
and the canonical always points home.

## 11. Before calling it done

- Every number in both posts traces to a source read this session or to `references/facts.md`.
- The answer block reads correctly with the rest of the page deleted.
- 4–5 FAQs on each post, each answer standing alone.
- No AI-generated image anywhere near the bulk post or LinkedIn.
- Both posts contain one scene and one piece of free advice.
- The LinkedIn hook survives the fold and the link is in the comment.
- `references/calendar.md` updated: topics marked shipped, next drop dated.
- Share card rendered, and the distribution list in `references/distribution.md`
  handed over with the drafts.
- The owner's summary at the end: what it means, what he must do, what it costs, what's waiting on him.
