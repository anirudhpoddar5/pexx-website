## Answer block

Most hand block printed orders take **45 to 60 days** from confirmation to
dispatch. The range moves with the quantity, the fabric and the weather. Cutting
new blocks takes 10 to 14 days and sits inside that window, not on top of it.
PEXX publishes no minimum order quantity — a first order is meant to be small
enough that you can sell it through.

We can usually tell how an email is going to go from the first line.

The ones that begin "I run a small store and I'm not sure if you work with
someone my size" are almost always from the people we most want to work with.
Someone who chose every product on her shelf herself. Who knows which print her
customers reach for and which one she over-ordered last year. Who is asking
because a supplier somewhere has already made her feel small.

So before anything else: yes, we work with you. Here are the two answers you're
actually after.

## How long does a hand block print order take?

Forty-five to sixty days, from a confirmed order to goods leaving. If your design
needs blocks that don't exist yet, the carving takes ten to fourteen days and it
happens inside that window, at the front — not stacked on the end.

Fifteen days is a wide gap and you're right to poke at it. Three things move it,
and you can usually tell in advance which one is going to move yours.

| What moves it | Which way | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Quantity** | more pieces, longer | Printing happens on a table, one length at a time. Table time is the one thing we can't run in parallel forever. |
| **Fabric** | voile fastest, quilted slowest | Voile drinks colour and dries quickly. Heavier cotton takes longer at both ends. Anything quilted goes off to be stitched after printing, so a quilt sits about a week behind a bedcover in the same print. |
| **Weather** | monsoon adds days | Printed cloth is washed and laid out to dry in the open. In a wet week the yard doesn't dry, and there's no version of us that can hurry that. |

If your date is genuinely fixed — a launch, a fair, a festival — say so in the
first email, not the fifth. We can nearly always build a plan around a date we
know about. There is very little we can do for one that turns up in week five.

And if you need goods sooner than that, ask what's already made. Running stock in
current prints ships far faster than something made to your design. Forty-five to
sixty days is the number for a print that doesn't exist yet.

## What is the minimum order quantity?

We don't publish one, and that's deliberate.

A first order isn't a sales figure to us, it's a trial run in both directions.
Did we understand what you actually meant, or only what you wrote? Is the quality
what we said it would be? Does somebody reply when you message on a Tuesday
afternoon?

You learn all of that on a small order just as well as on a large one, and only
one of those is comfortable to be wrong about.

We also know what a first order actually is. It's your own money, months ahead of
any of it selling, with a supplier you've never met, for stock you have already
imagined on the shelf. Start with what you can sell. That isn't us managing your
expectations — it's the order we'd place ourselves.

So tell us what you're making and roughly what you need. If it's too small to
make sensibly, we'll say so honestly and tell you why. That happens sometimes,
and it's a better conversation than a quote that quietly doesn't add up.

## What will I have to pay, and when?

Nobody enjoys asking this, so we'll go first: ask us in the very first email.
We'll tell you the advance, when the balance is due and whether sampling is
charged separately, before you commit to anything at all.

It's your money leaving months before anything comes back in. You're entitled to
know exactly when each part of it goes, in writing, without having to chase it.

## What if the sample doesn't look like what I pictured?

Almost nobody puts this in the email. Everybody thinks it. Say it out loud,
because this is the one stage where changing your mind costs nothing.

You'll see the print on your actual cloth, in your actual colours, before bulk
begins. When it arrives, don't approve it from your phone. Take it to a window.
Put it on a bed, or on the shelf beside the things you already sell. Photograph
it in the light your customer will see it in, because a colour that looks right
in a showroom can look wrong in a photo, and you'll be selling from photos.

Then say what's off, plainly. "Too yellow" tells us everything. So does "lovely,
but go a shade deeper". What doesn't help is "not quite there" — we'll guess, and
we'll guess wrong.

One thing worth settling before bulk: how much colour variation you're happy
with. Dye is mixed by hand and shifts a little between batches. That variation is
why the cloth looks alive instead of machine-printed. It is also, if nobody
agreed a range up front, the thing you end up arguing about while a customer
waits.

And if the print sells and you want it again — ask us to keep a swatch of the
approved batch. It costs nothing and it settles the question next season.

## What makes a block print order run late?

Almost never the printing.

It's a sample sitting unopened on somebody's desk for a fortnight. The clock
doesn't restart until it's approved, so two quiet weeks there become two weeks
off the far end — usually the two you needed.

So: before you send the enquiry, decide who signs off samples and give that
person a date. It'll save you more time than any amount of chasing later.

## How to get a block print order delivered faster

- Use prints and blocks that already exist. That takes the carving out of your
  timeline entirely.
- Tell us the delivery date you actually need, and what it's tied to.
- Send a photo of a colour you love, not just a code. We'll match to cloth.
- Say what the piece is for. A quilt for a nursery and a quilt for a hotel are
  built differently.
- Answer the sample the week it lands, even if the answer is "give me a week".

## FAQ

**How long does a hand block print order take?**
Most orders take 45 to 60 days from confirmation to dispatch. The range depends
on the quantity, the fabric being printed, and the weather, since printed cloth
is washed and dried in the open. Cutting new blocks takes 10 to 14 days and
happens inside that window.

**How long does it take to make new blocks?**
Ten to fourteen days. The motif is cut into seasoned wood by hand and the block
has to settle before it prints cleanly. Using prints that already exist removes
this step from your timeline.

**What is the minimum order quantity for block printed fabric?**
We don't publish one. A first order is where a buyer and a maker learn how the
other works, so we'd rather talk about what you're making and when you need it
than set a number you have to clear first.

**Why does the lead time vary between 45 and 60 days?**
Three things move it: how many pieces you're ordering, what they're printed on
(voile, heavier cotton and quilted pieces all behave differently), and the
weather, because cloth is dried in the open air after washing.

**Can you work with a custom design?**
Yes. A custom print needs its own blocks cut, which takes 10 to 14 days at the
start of the order. Send the artwork or a reference before sampling rather than
after, and tell us the scale you want it at.

**Can it carry my own label?**
Yes — woven labels, care labels and hangtags are stitched in at finishing. Send
the artwork or the labels themselves at sampling stage rather than after.

**Can you work with small independent brands?**
Yes. Many of the people we work with run their own stores and buy the quantities
they can actually sell. We'd rather grow with a buyer over several seasons than
push a large first order.
