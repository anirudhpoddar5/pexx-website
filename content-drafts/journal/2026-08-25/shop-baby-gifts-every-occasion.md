You know the pile. Every family has one.

Two months after the ceremony, half the gifts are still in their bags in the
corner of the room. Nobody chose badly. It's just that fourteen people made the
same sensible decision on the same afternoon, and there is only so much a newborn
can wear before growing out of it.

If you're the one standing in a shop this week trying not to add to that pile,
this is for you. It's written by people who make some of these things, and we'll
happily point you at the ones we don't.

## The one rule for choosing a baby gift: no sizes

**Give something that doesn't have a size.**

Apply that one line and most of the pile disappears before you've even walked
into a shop. What's left is the small handful of things that get picked up every
day for the next two years.

Newborn clothes are lovely and everyone buys them. Most babies are out of that
size within a month, sooner if they arrive on the bigger side, often while the
family is still receiving visitors. If your heart is set on clothes, buy 6–12
months instead. Hardly anyone buys ahead, so that's usually the empty drawer.

## The best gifts for a newborn baby in India

**A set of cotton swaddles.** If you give one thing, give these. A swaddle is a
nursing cover, a pram cover, a clean sheet on somebody else's bed, a shade over
the car seat, a floor blanket at a relative's house. It ends up being the thing
the toddler won't sleep without. They live in the wash, so a set of two is the
right unit — which is how ours come.

**A light cotton quilt.** Not a heavy winter one. A soft quilted cotton blanket
is what gets used on the floor during play, over a pram, in an air-conditioned
room. Worth knowing: quilts and blankets don't belong in the cot in the first
year — safe-sleep guidance is a flat, clear cot — but out of the cot it's the
piece most likely to still be in the house years later, by then as a den roof.

**A wash or nappy pouch.** Unromantic, used daily, and the thing most parents end
up buying for themselves in the first fortnight.

**Something for the mother.** Fourteen people are about to hand over something
for the baby. She is standing right there, three weeks into not sleeping,
watching gifts arrive for somebody else. Hand cream, for hands that are about to
be washed forty times a day. Something soft she can feed in. It costs the least
of anything on this list and it's the one she'll still mention two years later.

## What not to gift a newborn baby

- **Strong fragrances.** Newborn skin reacts to them, and plenty of new mothers
  are still queasy for weeks. It's the one gift that can genuinely make things
  worse.
- **Big soft toys.** They arrive by the dozen and go straight into storage.
- **Anything with small parts that come off,** even if it's meant for later.
- **Party outfits in newborn size.** Beautiful in one photograph, then never
  again.
- **Cot sets with pillows and bumpers.** Safe-sleep advice is a flat, clear cot
  in the first year — no pillows, bumpers or loose bedding.

## How to write a baby gift message

Write on the tag, not the box. And write to the mother, not to the baby.

The box goes out with the recycling that same week. The tag ends up taped inside
the back of the baby book, and it stays there.

If you're giving a swaddle or a quilt, either wash it first or say plainly that
it needs one. A line that says *give this a cold wash before use, it only gets
softer* is a small kindness in a month when everyone is telling that family a
hundred contradictory things.

## FAQ

**What should you gift a new baby in India?**
Things that don't depend on size and get used daily — cotton swaddles, a light
quilt, a wash or nappy pouch — plus something for the mother. Newborn clothing is
outgrown within about a month and most guests already bring it.

**What do you gift at a godh bharai?**
Give what the first weeks use up: a set of swaddles, a light cotton quilt, and
something for the mother. The baby isn't here yet, so practical multiples are
more useful than a single keepsake.

**What is a good gift for a naming ceremony?**
For a namkaran, something that photographs well and stays useful — a
block-printed quilt or swaddle in a print with character. It appears in the day's
photographs and goes back to daily use the next morning.

**What is the difference between namkaran and annaprashan?**
Namkaran is the naming ceremony, usually held within the first few weeks.
Annaprashan is the first-rice ceremony at around six months, when the baby is
given solid food for the first time. They are separate occasions about five
months apart.

**Is it alright to give clothes as a baby gift?**
Yes, but buy 6–12 month sizes rather than newborn. Most babies are out of newborn
sizing within a month, and it's the size every other guest buys.

**What should you avoid gifting a newborn?**
Strongly fragranced products, large soft toys, anything with small detachable
parts, newborn-size party outfits, and cot bedding sets with pillows or bumpers.
Safe-sleep guidance is a flat, clear cot for the first year.

**Can I send a baby gift to India from abroad?**
Yes. Order to an Indian address, add your message as a gift note, and it arrives
wrapped. Shipping is free over ₹750 and delivery covers India, so nothing has to
be shipped internationally. No price or invoice goes inside the parcel.

**Can I order baby gifts in bulk for an event?**

mixed, with a quote within a day.
