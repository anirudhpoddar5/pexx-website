## The post

Every buyer asks for our minimum order quantity. We don't publish one.

Not as a negotiating tactic. Because a first order isn't really about volume —
it's both sides finding out how the other works. Was the brief understood. Is the
quality what was promised. Do messages get answered on a Tuesday afternoon.

You learn all of that on 200 pieces just as well as on 2,000, and only one of
those is a comfortable amount to be wrong about.

What we do commit to is the date: deliveries start within 45 days, including
orders where the blocks don't exist yet and have to be carved.

Here's where those 45 days actually go, since almost nobody outside the trade
knows:

A carver cuts the motif into teak — a few hours for something simple, three or
four days for something fine. Then the block sits in oil for about a week before
it can print. Strike-off in ten to fourteen days. Pre-production sample, three
weeks. Then printing, one colour at a time, each pass waiting for the last one to
dry, and the cloth laid out in the sun afterwards.

And the honest part: almost every late delivery I've seen didn't go wrong in
printing. It went wrong in the middle — a sample sitting unapproved on somebody's
desk for a fortnight while the production clock waited.

So the one thing I'd offer any buyer, whether or not you ever work with us: name
the person who signs off the sample, and give them a date. That single line in
your process is worth more than any amount of chasing at the other end.
