## What does "skin-safe" actually mean on a baby product?

It isn't a certification, and on an Indian listing it isn't regulated. Used
honestly it means two things.

**The fibre.** Pure cotton or muslin. A polyester blend doesn't absorb sweat, so
it sits on the skin — which in a Chennai August is the direct route to heat rash.

**The dye.** A subset of azo dyes break down into aromatic amines classed as
hazardous. India prohibited 42 of them in 1993 and 70 more in 1997, under the
Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, and imports must comply too.

Neither is exotic or expensive. A seller who can't answer a plain question about
either is the signal, not the price.

## What size should a baby swaddle be?

About a metre square — ours are 100×100 cm. Much below that, a swaddle stops
working as a wrap within a couple of months and turns into a nursing cover.

Size matters more than thread count. A generous, slightly thin muslin wraps
longer, layers better in winter, and dries faster in monsoon.

## Muslin, cotton or flannel — which weave for which Indian season?

| Weave | Roughly | Right for | Wrong for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Muslin / mulmul** | 50–80 GSM per layer | Summer, humid cities, swaddling | A north Indian January alone |
| **Woven cotton, 200TC** | 100–140 GSM | Sheets and quilt covers | It's the workhorse — little |
| **Three-layer dohar** | 240–300 GSM total | Winter and AC rooms, toddler up | The cot, in the first year |
| **Flannel / brushed** | Heavier, napped | Hill stations, coldest weeks | Most of India, most of the year |
| **Synthetic comforter** | Varies | Very little, for a baby | Any warm room — it doesn't breathe |

A dohar is three cotton layers with no filling — two muslin outer, one inner.
Warm without being heavy, where a comforter would make a baby sweat.

## Five questions to send any seller before you buy

Copy this into a WhatsApp message, whoever you're buying from. A good seller
answers all five in one reply.

1. What's the fibre content — pure cotton, or a blend?
2. Are the dyes azo-free?
3. What's the finished size after washing, not before?
4. Does it need a separate first wash, and will the colour bleed?
5. What's the return window if it turns out not to suit?

Vague on question two is your answer. Vague on question four and you'll find out
the hard way, in the machine.

## What should never go in a cot

- **Pillows and bolsters** in the first year.
- **Cot bumpers**, padded or mesh.
- **Loose quilts and blankets** over a sleeping baby.
- **Soft toys** in the sleep space.
- **Anything with ribbons, ties or cords** near the sleep surface.

The cot is a firm flat mattress, a fitted sheet, and the baby on their back.
Everything else moves to the floor and the pram, where it gets more use anyway.

## FAQ

**What is the safest fabric for baby bedding in India?**
Pure cotton or muslin, coloured with azo-free dyes. Both breathe, which matters
most in Indian heat and humidity, and neither traps sweat against the skin the
way a polyester blend does.

**What size should a baby swaddle be?**
About a metre square — ours are 100×100 cm. Smaller squares stop working as wraps within a
couple of months, though they remain useful as nursing or pram covers.

**Are cot bumpers and baby pillows safe?**
They are widely sold in India but are not recommended for the first year. Safe
sleep is a firm flat mattress with a fitted sheet and nothing else in the cot.

**Is muslin warm enough for winter?**
One layer isn't, in a north Indian winter. Muslin layers well, so use two, or
switch to a three-layer cotton dohar at 240–300 GSM — used outside the cot for
babies under one.

**What does "azo-free dye" mean?**
It means the fabric is coloured without the specific azo dyes that can release
hazardous aromatic amines. India has prohibited handling those dyes since the
1990s, so it's a claim a seller should be able to confirm without hesitating.
