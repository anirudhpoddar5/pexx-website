The best non-plastic return gifts are things a child uses again: cotton pouches
and pencil cases, wooden toys, books, art supplies, steel snack boxes, seed

## Why non-plastic return gifts land better

Parents are the second audience at every children's party, and they decide what
survives the car ride home. A gift that gets used — carried to school, kept on a
desk, eaten — earns a real thank-you that evening. A thin plastic toy with a
moving part is one drop from being rubbish, and the broken pieces are exactly
the hazard you didn't want at a party full of under-fives.

## 12 return gift ideas for kids that aren't plastic

**The ones that get used all year**

1. **Block-printed cotton pouch** — pencils, coins, hairbands, a whole school
   year of small things. The single most-used return gift there is.
2. **Fabric pencil case** — goes into a school bag the following Monday and
   stays there.
3. **Small drawstring potli** — the favour bag and the gift in one, which also
   saves you the wrapping.
4. **Cloth book sleeve or library bag** — good for any school with a reading day.
5. **Cotton handkerchief or napkin set** — unfashionable, used constantly, easy
   to put a name on.
6. **A small backpack** — for a short guest list, where one real thing beats
   thirty small ones.

**Good non-fabric options, if you'd rather**

7. **Wooden or beeswax crayons** — no plastic casing, easier in small hands.
8. **A slim sketchbook** — pairs with almost anything else on this list.
9. **A wooden puzzle or a traditional toy** — a lattu, a spinning top, a

10. **Seed pencils or a seed-paper card** — the child plants the stub, and it
    gets talked about at school.

The ones that work all have a second life. The bag after the cookies, the pouch
for the next four years.

## Should you give different gifts to different ages?

Usually not — children compare immediately and out loud, so one gift for
everybody keeps the peace. Split only for a real age gap, and make the two look
as similar as you can.

Safety is the exception that isn't optional.

| Guest age | Safe to give | Skip |
|---|---|---|
| **Under 3** | Cloth pouches, chunky wooden pieces, parent-approved edibles | Small detachable parts, beads, buttons, cords, ribbons |
| **3–6** | Crayons, clay, cloth items, simple wooden toys, seed pencils | Small magnets, sharp edges, tiny loose parts |
| **7–10** | Pencil cases, sketchbooks, card games, book sleeves | Babyish items — this age notices |
| **10+** | Stationery, a bottle, a snack box, a small game | Anything that reads as a toddler favour |

If the guest list crosses under-3, use the under-3 row for everybody. It costs
nothing and removes the one real risk on the table.

## The free advice: put the name on the tag, not on the gift

The expensive mistake at this scale is personalising the object itself. Thirty
pouches, each with a different name printed on the cloth: it looks wonderful in
the plan, costs more, arrives with two names spelled wrong, and can't be reused
when a guest doesn't come.

Put the name on a tag and tie it on. Just as personal, and every spare stays
usable.

Two more that cost nothing:

- **Hand them out at the door as children leave.** A gift given at the beginning
  is opened, compared and abandoned before the cake.
- **Wrap them all in one sitting**, identically, two days before. Thirty
  identical parcels take forty minutes. Thirty individual ones take a Saturday.

Cloth items, chunky wooden pieces and parent-approved edibles. Avoid small
detachable parts, beads, buttons, magnets and long cords — and if any under-3s
are coming, apply that standard to everyone.
