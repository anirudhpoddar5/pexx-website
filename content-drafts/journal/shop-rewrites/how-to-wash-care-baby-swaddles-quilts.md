## Answer block

Wash baby swaddles and quilts in cold or lukewarm water on a gentle cycle with a
mild detergent, turned inside out, and dry in shade. No bleach, no fabric
softener, no tumble drying. Wash new hand block-printed pieces separately for
the first two or three washes — a little colour release is normal.

---

The first time a block-printed quilt bleeds in the wash, everybody thinks the
same thing. It's ruined. Somebody sold me something that isn't colourfast.

It hasn't, and they haven't. Hand-printed cotton carries a small amount of
surface colour that was never going to stay, and the first wash is where it
leaves. It just needs to leave into a bucket rather than into a white cot sheet.

Everything below keeps these pieces soft and printed for years, and none of it
is specific to what we make.

## How do you wash a baby swaddle or quilt? The short version

1. **First wash, alone.** Cold water, mild detergent, nothing else in the drum.
   Repeat for the first two or three washes on a hand-printed piece.
2. **Turn it inside out.** The print faces away from the drum and the sun.
3. **Cold or lukewarm, gentle cycle, half a scoop.** Hot water sets stains and
   dulls colour, and extra detergent doesn't rinse out.
4. **Skip the softener.** Explained below.
5. **Extra rinse.** The best single thing you can do for skin that reacts.
6. **Dry in shade**, not in direct sun.
7. **Fold, don't hang, for storage.**

It takes longer to read than to do.

## Should you wash a new swaddle before using it?

Yes. A new piece has been through a mill, a print table, a folding room and a
parcel. A cold wash clears the finish, softens the weave, and gives loose surface
colour somewhere to go — a bucket, not a load of white vests.

If you're giving one, put that on the tag: *give this a cold wash before use, it
only gets softer.* Half the gifted baby textiles in India sit unused because
nobody said whether they were ready.

## Why not fabric softener?

Softener works by coating the fibre, and cotton doing its job depends on the
fibre being uncoated. That coating reduces absorbency — the same reason towels
stop drying you after a few softener washes — and it builds up. On a swaddle,
absorbency is half the point.

Cotton and mulmul soften on their own, permanently, which a coating never does.
Same for bleach and heavily fragranced detergent: if the wash leaves a smell in
the room, it's leaving something on the cloth too.

## Colour bleeding: what's normal and what isn't

| What you see | Normal? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Faint tint in the first wash water | Yes | Wash separately two or three times, then treat as ordinary cotton |
| Still releasing lightly at wash five or six | Yes on deep indigo, red, black | Keep washing separately, cold, no soaking |
| Colour transferred onto a white item | Yes, and avoidable | Rewash the white item before it dries |
| Print cracking or flaking in patches | No | That's a surface pigment print. Contact the seller |
| Whole piece pale and uneven | No | Usually sun damage from drying in direct light |

Deep colours release more, and heat and soaking make it worse. Cold water and a
short cycle keep it contained.

## How to dry cotton and block print — including in monsoon

Dry in shade. Direct Indian sun fades printed cotton unevenly, and the fold line
shows it first — a pale stripe across a quilt that no washing brings back. Shade,
moving air, turn it once.

In monsoon the enemy is a piece drying slowly enough to go musty. Wring gently,
roll it in a dry towel, hang it with air on both sides rather than doubled over a
line, and put a fan on it.

Never tumble dry a hand-printed quilt, and never dry-clean one. The solvent is
harsher on natural dye than anything you'd do at home.

## What works on baby stains

- **Milk, formula, spit-up.** Protein. Cold water only, immediately. Hot water
  cooks it into the fibre and it's permanent.
- **Nappy leaks.** Rinse cold, then soak in cold water with mild detergent. No
  oxygen bleach on a printed piece.
- **Turmeric, tomato, carrot.** Rinse cold, then dry in indirect daylight.
  Turmeric fades with light over a few days. Keep harsh sun off the print.
- **Oil.** Blot, don't rub. Mild detergent worked in with a fingertip, then a
  cold wash.

The habit that beats all of them: get to it while it's wet.

## Storing quilts and swaddles between seasons

Wash and dry completely first — anything put away damp comes out with a smell
that takes several washes to shift. Fold rather than hang; a hanger
stretches woven cotton at the shoulder and the weave doesn't come back. Store in
cotton or a cloth bag, not sealed plastic. Yellowing is usually residue
oxidising rather than dirt, and a cold wash with a proper rinse handles most.

## The free advice: use half the detergent and add a rinse

Two changes, no cost, and they work on every load in the house.

**Halve the detergent.** Almost everyone over-doses, and modern machines use less
water than the doses assume. What doesn't rinse away stays in the
fibre, stiffens the cloth, and is a common cause of skin reacting to something
washed "properly".

**Add the extra rinse.** If a baby's skin is reacting and you've changed
detergent twice already, the problem is usually the rinse, not the brand.

## FAQ

**How do you wash a baby swaddle?**
Cold or lukewarm water, gentle cycle, mild detergent, turned inside out, then
dry in shade. Wash new swaddles once before first use, and keep hand block
printed pieces separate for the first two or three washes.

**Is it normal for a block print to bleed in the wash?**
Yes, lightly, in the first few washes, and more on deep indigo, red and black.
It settles quickly. Print that cracks or flakes off in patches is a different
problem and worth raising with the seller.

**Can you machine wash a hand block printed quilt?**
Yes, on a cold gentle cycle, inside out, with a mild detergent. Avoid hot water,
bleach, tumble drying and dry cleaning, all of which are harder on natural dye
than the machine is.

**Why should you avoid fabric softener on baby fabrics?**
Softener coats the fibre, which reduces cotton's absorbency over repeated
washes. Cotton and mulmul soften naturally with every wash, so the softener is
working against the thing you want.

**Can you dry baby quilts in the sun?**
Dry in shade. Direct sun fades printed cotton unevenly, and fold lines are where
it shows first. Shade with moving air dries just as well without the bleaching.

**How should you store quilts and swaddles between seasons?**
Fully dry, folded rather than hung, in cotton or a cloth bag rather than sealed
plastic. Hanging stretches the weave and plastic traps moisture against the
fibre.
