# How luxury houses actually write — verbatim evidence

Collected 23 Aug 2026 from brands' own sites (Ralph Lauren AU, Chanel editorial),
Dior's own pages via the Internet Archive, and Bottega's press release reproduced
by a stockist. Instagram captions were unobtainable for all four — login-walled.
Nothing below is paraphrased.

## The finding that matters most

**Luxury copy is not vague or mysterious. It is unusually SPECIFIC.**
The register is restrained, but the content is dense with hard facts — hours,
dates, place names, fibre names, job titles. What is removed is persuasion, not
information.

- Ralph Lauren: "takes up to eight hours to construct"
- Ralph Lauren: "custom-developed using linen, mulberry silk and wool"
- Chanel: "shoemakers, last makers, cutters, pattern makers, stitchers and
  finishers at Massaro"
- Chanel: "The collaboration between CHANEL and Massaro began in 1957"
- Bottega: "First introduced in 1975"
- Dior: "Centifolia rose, with its honeyed and peppery notes, reigns over a
  creamy velvet heart"

This is the opposite of the poetic-vagueness trap. "Some gifts are opened once"
is a greeting card. "Sixty impressions of a wooden block" is luxury.

## The rules, with evidence

**1. Describe, never persuade.** Dior's fragrance copy is pure description —
no benefit claim, no "you will feel". The reader is trusted to conclude.

**2. Specificity replaces adjectives.** Nobody writes "exceptional quality".
They write the number of hours, the name of the workshop, the year it began.

**3. Headlines are short declarative fragments.**
"Craft is our Language." / "Every bag has a story." / "Timeless by Design." /
"Codes on-the-go." / "IMMEASURABLY INTENSE."

**4. Origin is dated.** "In 1947, Miss Dior was born…" / "First introduced in
1975" / "began in 1957". A date is the cheapest credibility there is.

**5. Artisans are named with respect, never as a selling point.**
Chanel: "a declaration of love to the artisans who contribute to CHANEL
collections all year round." Note it is affection, not provenance-marketing.

**6. Metaphor is allowed, but must be anchored to the object.**
Bottega: "two different strips woven together that become stronger — the two
things make a stronger whole."

**7. Never justify price.** Not one of the four does it, anywhere.

**8. Almost no second person, no imperatives.** The exception is "Discover".

## The outlier worth studying — Ralph Lauren

RL is warmer and more narrative than the European houses, and closest to what a
family-run Indian textile house could credibly sound like:

> "Style is very personal. It has nothing to do with fashion. Fashion is over
> very quickly. Style is forever."

> "In my mind, I create a world based around the girl. When I design a
> collection, I have that heroine in mind. She is the star of my movie."

> "For me, luxury is a sensibility, an approach to life. It's not about the
> season's newest anything. It's about personal style and creating an
> environment of comfort and ease. Luxury is quality and timeless elegance."

> "The Pickup Truck, worn and weathered, evokes the honesty of hard work."
> "The Barn, forever a metaphor for utility, purpose, and community."

RL sells a **world**; Loro Piana sells a **material**; Cucinelli sells a
**belief**; Bottega sells a **technique**. Choosing which of these PEXX is
determines every headline.

## What this means for PEXX

The failure mode to avoid is not "too much information" — it is persuasion and
generality. Write the specific fact, plainly, and stop.

Bad:  "Some gifts are opened once."
Good: "Sixty impressions of a wooden block. Four colours. Four days of drying."

The palette work supports this directly: a named colour is a specific, and
specifics are the raw material of this register.
