# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-07-22

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** PEXX is a D2C Shopify brand selling hand block-printed kids textiles and accessories, made in Jaipur, priced and positioned for Indian gifting occasions.
**What it does:** Sells quilts, swaddles, dohars, backpacks, pouches, toiletry kits, and totes — each hand block-printed by a named artisan — direct to Indian consumers, with a companion NRI "Gift to India" hub for diaspora buyers.
**Product category:** D2C kids merchandise & accessories (gifting-first), block-print textile craft
**Product type:** E-commerce (Shopify)
**Business model:** Direct sale, INR pricing, no discount-led promotions — scarcity (limited print runs, seasonal collections) instead of price cuts.

## Target Audience
**Target customers:** Parents and gift-givers in urban India buying for babies/kids; secondary — NRI diaspora sending gifts to family in India.
**Primary use case:** Finding a meaningful, higher-quality gift for a specific Indian occasion rather than a generic mass-market baby item.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Buy a gift for a baby shower, naming ceremony, homecoming, or birthday that feels considered, not generic
- Send a culturally-resonant, craft-backed gift to family in India from abroad (NRI)
- Give a corporate/bulk gift with an authentic craft story attached
**Use cases:**
- Individual gifting (baby shower, naming ceremony, Rakhi, Diwali)
- NRI gift-to-India orders
- Bulk/corporate gifting (wedding favors, corporate gifting — per earlier gifting-page strategy work)

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Existing options force a tradeoff — mass-market brands (Kassy Pop, SuperBottoms) are discount-heavy with no craft story; design-led organic brands (Masilo, The Baby Atelier) are GOTS-certified but hide the artisan/craft process; the one brand doing this well (Malabar Baby) is USD-priced and not built for Indian occasions.
**Why alternatives fall short:** No Indian brand combines domestic INR pricing + visible block-print craft + Indian gifting-occasion framing.
**Emotional tension:** Wanting a gift that feels meaningful and not mass-produced, without paying import prices or settling for generic "handmade with love" copy that every brand uses.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Malabar Baby (US) — same craft positioning (hand block-print, GOTS, gifting-first) but USD-priced ($50–$418 sets) and not built around Indian occasions.
**Cluster A — Mass functional:** Kassy Pop, SuperBottoms — discount-heavy, no artisan angle.
**Cluster B — Design-led organic:** Masilo, The Baby Atelier — GOTS-certified, modern aesthetic, but craft process is invisible.
**Gap PEXX fills (Cluster D):** Domestic INR pricing + block-print craft transparency + Indian gifting occasions — no Indian brand currently holds this position.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Artisan named and shown at the product level (name, face, short video) — not buried in an About page
- Never discounts — uses scarcity (limited print runs, named seasonal collections) instead
- Block print for kids treated as a focused identity, not an afterthought to adult wear
- Owns Indian gifting occasions specifically — baby shower, naming ceremony, homecoming, Diwali, Rakhi — with price-tiered curated bundles (₹1,500 / ₹3,500 / ₹7,000)
- Price transparency — a "Cost of a Block Print" breakdown pre-empts Amazon price comparisons
- Packaging treated as product — block-printed gift wrap/box + artisan story card in every gift order
- Pursuing GOTS + OEKO-TEX Standard 100 Class I together (no Indian brand currently holds both)
**Why customers choose PEXX:** Same craft depth and gifting focus as Malabar Baby, but priced and occasion-tailored for the Indian market — a lane currently empty.

## Anti-Patterns (What We Will NOT Do)
- No permanent discount sales (erodes brand equity — see Aachho, Tjori as cautionary examples)
- No discount-first homepage
- No generic "handmade with love" copy — every competitor already says this
- No age-restrictive swaddle copy ("only for newborns") — show 5+ uses to extend perceived product life
