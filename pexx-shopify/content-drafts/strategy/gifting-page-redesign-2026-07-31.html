PEXX Gifting Redesign — Design Spec

Written 2026-07-31. Supersedes/extends gifting-page-occasion-strategy-2026-07-17.html (IA rationale and per-function wedding breakdown from that doc still hold — this doc adds layout, cross-page repetition strategy, the enquiry form redesign, page naming, and photo assignment now that real product photography exists). Decided in direct conversation with the founder plus one independent Opus review pass on the whole plan (design/SEO/AEO/naming).

Four occasion pages — Corporate, Weddings, Kids, Festive/General — each treated as a complete, standalone page for an emotional purchase, not a thin tile pointing elsewhere. This doc is the structure, the shared components, the final copy naming, and the photo-to-section plan for the 22 real photos now available (Kids has zero — still blocked on the merchant).

Nav mega-menu links directly to all 4 occasion pages — one click each, no forced stop at a hub. /pages/gifting stays, but stops being a gatekeeper and becomes a real page: brand philosophy, one photo + brief explainer per category with a "see more" link into the full page, and its own enquiry form (the only page where the Occasion field is a visible dropdown, since it serves every occasion at once).

2. Shared page template

All 4 occasion pages (and conceptually the hub) share the same structural skeleton, top to bottom:

  1. Eyebrow + H1 — brand-voice, not a flat category label (final copy in §4).
  2. Intro — 2–3 sentences, occasion-specific.
  3. Trust/process band — identical structure across all pages (§2.1).
  4. Category sections — zig-zag layout, behavior-ordered not alphabetical (§2.2).
  5. Enquiry form — one per page, shared component (§3).
  6. Cross-occasion carousel — bottom of page, all 4 pages (§2.3).

2.1 Trust/process band

Same short block, same order, on every page. No public MOQ, delivery-date, or price numbers anywhere in it — those are captured through the enquiry form instead (quantity/timeline/budget fields), not published as page copy.

  1. Lead point — design collaboration. "We design this with you — colours, prints, even a co-branded fabric label reading PEXX × [Your Company] — down to your minimum order. Smaller batch? Choose straight from our print library instead." This is the #1 differentiator vs. generic bulk-gifting vendors and should open the band, not trail it.
  2. One seamless process, start to finish — a single point of contact, not five vendor calls.
  3. Committed delivery — dates that hold, stated in principle, not as a specific number on-page.
  4. Consistent quality — piece 1 and piece 500 are the same piece.

PEXX × [Your Company] stays scoped to this one feature — it does not become a page name or brand identity elsewhere (Opus's point, unchallenged: it only parses for Corporate, and a bracketed placeholder in an H1 reads as unfinished).

2.2 Category sections — zig-zag layout

  • Alternating image-left/text-right, then image-right/text-left, section by section. Image ~55–60% width — it carries the emotional weight before any copy is read.
  • Copy capped at 2–3 sentences per section, written as a small story (the anxiety this replaces → what PEXX gives instead), not a bulleted spec sheet. "Hand block-printed, azo-free, customizable" as a bullet list reads like a spec page, not a gift.
  • Section order is behavioral, not alphabetical — see §5 for the actual order per page. The Wedding page specifically runs in the real chronological sequence of wedding functions rather than leading with whichever concept is our favorite.
  • Kids at the Wedding keeps its chronological body position (not pulled to the top of the grid), but its concept and best photo are hoisted into the Wedding page's hero — one line above the trust band — so the differentiator isn't buried just because chronology doesn't put it first.

2.3 Cross-occasion carousel

A photo-card strip at the bottom of all 4 pages, surfacing content that actually lives on other pages (e.g. a Corporate-page visitor sees a "Festive Hampers" card without hunting through nav).

Click behavior — real navigation, not a same-page form-field swap. A card links to /pages/gifting-festive?occasion=...#enquiry — the existing prefill mechanism, but pointed at the real page. Reasoning (confirmed after review): a same-page swap means someone enquires for "Festive Hampers" without ever seeing a festive photo or the festive-specific pitch — weaker lead, broken promise on the label they clicked. Real navigation shows the matching page's own trust band and story first, then asks for the form — better-primed lead, still one click, still prefilled.

Card set per page reuses whichever photos live on the other pages — not new photography, just a second placement of an existing image with its own occasion label. Duplication of these small card images/captions across all 5 pages is expected site furniture, not an SEO thin-content risk on its own (see §6).

Weddings and Festive H1s are new: the old Wedding line ("One less thing to get wrong") read as risk-avoidance rather than elevation; the old Festive line ("Sorted by October. Sleep through November.") pinned the whole page to a Diwali/October calendar moment when it needs to cover housewarmings, anniversaries, and adult-birthday return gifts year-round.

SEO keyword coverage happens below the H1, not inside it: a small literal subhead line (e.g. "Corporate gifting, hand block-printed in Jaipur" / "Birthday return gifts, azo-free cotton") plus the page's <title> tag carries the head term. This is the mechanism that lets the H1 stay emotional without losing search relevance.

6. SEO

  • Flat IA confirmed sound — 4 pages, one click from nav, no forced hierarchy.
  • Carousel duplication (same small photo+label repeated across 5 pages) is normal site furniture, not a thin-content risk on its own.
  • The real thin-content risk is word-count ratio: 2–3 sentences × ~7 sections is roughly 250–350 words of genuinely unique prose per page, against an identical trust band, form, and carousel. Fix: each occasion page carries its own small FAQ accordion (4–6 Q&As, unique per page — MOQ-shape questions, lead time, logo/co-branding, care instructions) — skimmable, opt-in, doesn't bloat the primary emotional read, but hits the word-count and structured-fact bar. Actual FAQ copy is a later copywriting pass, not decided in this doc.
  • The hub page needs to carry content that exists nowhere else to avoid reading as a thin duplicate of the 4 real pages — the block-print process, Jaipur provenance, azo-free sourcing, and the broadest FAQ set live there.
  • Linking pattern: hub → all 4 with keyword anchor text; all 4 → hub via breadcrumb; all 4 → each other via the real-navigation carousel.

7. AEO (answer-engine citability)

  • FAQPage schema on all 5 pages, matching the visible FAQ accordion text exactly.
  • Service schema per occasion page (serviceType, areaServed: IN, provider → Organization), plus one sitewide Organization/LocalBusiness block with Jaipur address, and BreadcrumbList on all 4 occasion pages.
  • Skip HowTo schema for the process band — Google retired HowTo rich results; express the process as a visible ordered list plus one FAQ entry instead.
  • No Product schema on these pages — no price/offer to attach, that belongs on real PDPs only.
  • Skip llms.txt — no major answer engine honors it today. Better use of effort: confirm robots.txt doesn't block GPTBot/PerplexityBot/ClaudeBot, and keep every material fact in real HTML text rather than baked into photos (a real risk given how photo-dominant this layout is).

8. USP messaging library

Reference list to draw from inside zig-zag mini-stories — not a bullet dump on any single page.

  • Away from mithai tins and pleather nobody fully trusts or keeps
  • Real hand block-print craft, made in Jaipur, not mass-factory output
  • Full custom design — colours, prints, a co-branded label — down to a low MOQ (starting ~10 pieces) vs. typical bulk vendors' 500+
  • Gifts people actually use and keep — recall value, not a re-gift or a bin item
  • Azo-free, child-safe cotton (leads for Kids and Wedding-kids audiences specifically)
  • One vendor, one seamless process, real committed delivery dates
  • Consistent quality at any batch size — piece 1 and piece 500 are the same piece
  • Reusable cotton vs. single-use synthetic favour bags or plastic wrap (mild sustainability angle, not a headline claim)
